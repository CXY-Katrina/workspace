## 模型完整原始回答

```text
The glass is moving at velocity v toward the observer, in the same direction as the beam. The light beam propagates in the same direction as the glass motion (toward the observer).

In the glass rest frame, the speed of light is c/n = 1/n. Using relativistic velocity addition (Lorentz transformation), the speed in the lab frame is:

u = (u' + v)/(1 + u' v/c²) = (1/n + v)/(1 + v/n) = (1 + n v)/(n + v).

So the answer is D.

Check limiting: v=0 → 1/n. Correct. As v → 1, u → (1+n)/(n+1) = 1. Correct, since light speed in vacuum is 1 and a moving medium... well, actually the light speed in the lab approaches 1 as the glass approaches light speed, which is consistent.

Answer: D.

**Relativistic velocity addition:** Since the glass moves at $v$ in the lab frame in the *same* direction as the beam, the lab-frame speed is

$$u = \frac{u' + v}{1 + u'v/c^2} = \frac{1/n + v}{1 + v/n}$$

**Simplifying:** Multiply numerator and denominator by $n$:

$$u = \frac{1 + nv}{n + v}$$

**Sanity checks:**

- $v \to 1$: gives $u \to (1+n)/(n+1) = 1$ ✓ (a medium moving at light speed drags its light along at $c$)
```
