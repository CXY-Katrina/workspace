The situation describes the **Liénard–Wiechert potentials** of a uniformly moving point charge, evaluated at the retarded time $t_r$.

**Setting up:** Let $\vec{d}$ point from the *retarded* source position (the position at time $t_r$) to the observation point $\vec{r}$, so $d = |\vec{d}|$ is the retarded distance, and the unit vector $\hat{n} = \vec{d}/d$.

**Scalar potential:** The LW potential is

$$V(\vec r,t) = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-\hat n\cdot\vec\beta)\,d_{ret}},\qquad \vec\beta = \frac{\vec v}{c}$$

Since $\hat n\cdot\vec v = \frac{\vec d\cdot\vec v}{d}$, the combination appearing in the denominator is

$$d_{ret}\left(1-\frac{\vec d\cdot\vec v}{cd}\right) = d - \frac{\vec d\cdot\vec v}{c} = \frac{cd - \vec d\cdot\vec v}{c}$$

Therefore

$$V(\vec r,t) = \frac{q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\cdot\frac{c}{cd-\vec d\cdot\vec v} = \frac{qc}{4\pi\epsilon_0\,(dc-\vec d\cdot\vec v)}$$

**Vector potential:** The vector potential is $\vec A = \dfrac{\vec v}{c^2}V$, so

$$\vec A(\vec r,t) = \frac{qc\,\vec v}{4\pi\epsilon_0 c^2 (dc-\vec d\cdot\vec v)} = \frac{qc\,\vec v}{4\pi \mu_0 (dc-\vec d\cdot\vec v)}$$

using $\mu_0\epsilon_0 c^2 = 1 \Rightarrow \frac{1}{\epsilon_0 c} = \mu_0$.

**Checking options:**
- A, D: use the naive (non-retarded) Coulomb potential $q/(4\pi\epsilon_0 r)$ — wrong, they ignore retardation and the $(1-\hat n\cdot\vec\beta)$ factor.
- B: has the plus sign $dc+\vec d\cdot\vec v$ — wrong sign (that would correspond to the *advanced* solution).
- C: correct denominator $(dc-\vec d\cdot\vec v)$ and correct $\mu_0 qc\vec v$ numerator for $\vec A$.
