Answer: A.</think>## Setting up the physics

For a planet of mass $m_p$ on a circular orbit around a star of mass $M_*$, the radial velocity semi‑amplitude is

$$K = \left(\frac{2\pi G}{P}\right)^{1/3}\frac{m_p \sin i}{(M_*+m_p)^{2/3}} \approx \frac{c\,\Delta\lambda}{\lambda}$$

**Key point:** both systems have the same planet mass (Neptune‑like) and the same host star mass (Sun‑like), and both orbits are circular. So the only thing that changes between the two systems is the period:

$$K \propto P^{-1/3}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P \propto K^{-3}$$

## Applying the numbers

The measured shifts are converted to velocities via $K = c\,\Delta\lambda/\lambda$, and since it is the *same* spectral line at the *same* wavelength for both stars, the velocity ratio is just the shift ratio:

$$\frac{K_2}{K_1} = \frac{7}{5}$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{K_1}{K_2}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{5}{7}\right)^3 = \frac{125}{343} \approx 0.36$$

So planet #2's orbital period is about 0.36 times that of planet #1 (i.e., it orbits faster, consistent with having the larger Doppler amplitude).
