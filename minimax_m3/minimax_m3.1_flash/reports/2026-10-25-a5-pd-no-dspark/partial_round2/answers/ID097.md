In gametogenesis, the germline undergoes two cycles of meiosis to give rise to haploid gametes. The goal of meiosis, unlike mitosis, is to separate not only the replicated sister chromatids but also the homologous chromosome pairs to form haploid cells. Fertilized gametes enter embryogenesis and ultimately develop into an organism. Meiosis is not always perfectly executed, and most such cases result in embryonic lethality. Which molecular mechanism is responsible for less prominent phenotypic consequences of Klinefelter's syndrome compared to better known Down's syndrome?

A) chiasmata resolution by separase in diakinesis

## 模型完整原始回答

```text
The question asks about Klinefelter's syndrome (47,XXY) and why it has less prominent phenotypic consequences compared to Down's syndrome (trisomy 21). The key mechanism is X-inactivation (lyonization) — dosage compensation. The extra X chromosome is largely inactivated through chromatin modification — histone methyltransferases (e.g., XIST recruits PRC2, H3K27me3 methylation) leading to heterochromatin formation. So answer D: chromatin methylation by histone methyltransferases in the post-zygote.

Let's check options:
A) chiasmata resolution by separase in diakinesis — this is about homologous recombination and meiosis I segregation; not related to phenotypic consequences.
B) progression of the polymerase alpha in the morula/blastocyst — DNA replication in early embryo; not relevant.
C) attachment of spindle to kinetochores in the metaphase I — meiotic nondisjunction mechanism (the cause of aneuploidy), not the reason for less prominent phenotype.
D) chromatin methylation by histone methyltransferases in the post-zygote — X-inactivation via XIST/PRC2/H3K27me3 and histone modifications silencing the extra X. Correct.

So answer D.

Step-by-step reasoning: Klinefelter syndrome = 47,XXY, one extra X. Dosage compensation via X-inactivation in the post-zygotic stage silences the extra X, so phenotypic consequences are milder than autosomal trisomies (Down syndrome) where gene dosage cannot be compensated. The molecular mechanism: XIST transcript from the X chromosome to be inactivated recruits PRC2 (histone methyltransferase) which methylates H3K27, establishing heterochromatin and Barr body.

Answer: D.</think>**Reasoning:**

- Klinefelter's syndrome (47,XXY) results from meiotic nondisjunction, but the *milder* phenotype compared to Down's syndrome (trisomy 21) is explained by **dosage compensation via X-inactivation (lyonization)**.
- In the early post-zygotic embryo, the cell counts its X chromosomes. When more than one X is present, *XIST* (a long non-coding RNA) is transcribed from the surplus X and recruits Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2), whose histone methyltransferase activity (e.g., H3K27 trimethylation) converts the chromosome into transcriptionally silent heterochromatin (the Barr body). Thus ~95% of genes on the extra X are silenced, leaving the gene dosage similar to a normal XX male.
- Down's syndrome involves an extra copy of an entire autosome (chromosome 21); no such silencing mechanism exists for autosomes, so all three copies are expressed, producing a more prominent phenotype.
- Option A (separase/chiasmata resolution) and option C (spindle–kinetochore attachment in metaphase I) relate to the *origin* of the aneuploidy (nondisjunction), not to phenotype severity. Option B (polymerase alpha in morula/blastocyst) concerns DNA replication in early cleavage, not dosage compensation.

Therefore, the mechanism responsible for the less prominent phenotype is epigenetic silencing of the extra X through histone methylation.

Answer: D
```
